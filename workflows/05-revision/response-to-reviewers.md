# Response to Reviewers — Point-by-Point Drafting

## Purpose

Generate a comprehensive, professional point-by-point response to reviewer and editor comments. Every single comment must be addressed — none may be ignored.

---

## Response Patterns

### Pattern A: Agree and Revise
> **Reviewer**: The authors should include a sensitivity analysis for the discount rate.
>
> We thank the reviewer for this suggestion. We have now included a sensitivity analysis varying the discount rate from 0% to 5%, with results presented in Supplementary Table S3 (Page 15, Lines 312-320). The main conclusions remain robust across all tested discount rates.

### Pattern B: Partially Agree
> **Reviewer**: The sample size appears too small for the analysis conducted.
>
> We appreciate this concern. While we acknowledge the sample size is modest (n=150), we have now added a post-hoc power analysis (Page 8, Lines 167-172) demonstrating 82% power to detect the observed effect size. We have also added a discussion of sample size as a limitation (Page 18, Lines 378-382). We note that our sample size is comparable to similar studies in this field (Chen et al., 2023; Williams et al., 2024).

### Pattern C: Respectfully Disagree
> **Reviewer**: The authors should use propensity score matching instead of multivariable regression.
>
> We thank the reviewer for this methodological suggestion. After careful consideration, we respectfully maintain our use of multivariable regression for the following reasons: (1) our sample size is relatively small, and propensity score matching would substantially reduce the effective sample; (2) multivariable regression is the standard approach in health economic evaluations (Drummond et al., 2015); (3) we have verified that all model assumptions are met (Supplementary Table S2). However, we have added a discussion of alternative analytical approaches including propensity score matching as a consideration for future research (Page 18, Lines 370-375).

### Pattern D: Additional Analysis Required
> **Reviewer**: The authors should stratify results by urban versus rural settings.
>
> This is an excellent suggestion. We have now conducted a stratified analysis by setting (urban vs. rural), with results presented in Table 3 (new) and discussed on Page 14, Lines 290-305. The stratified analysis reveals that the intervention was more cost-effective in rural settings (ICER: $12,500/QALY) compared to urban settings (ICER: $28,000/QALY), likely due to higher baseline travel costs in rural areas.

## Tone Guidelines

1. **Always thank reviewers** for their time and specific comments
2. **Be professional and respectful**, even when disagreeing
3. **Be specific** about what was changed and where
4. **Provide evidence** when disagreeing (citations, data, methodological standards)
5. **Never be defensive or dismissive** — reviewers are giving their time voluntarily
6. **Acknowledge limitations** honestly when raised
7. **Be concise** but complete — address every point fully

---

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

1. **Missing a comment**: Every single reviewer point must be addressed, no matter how minor
2. **Vague responses**: "We have revised the manuscript accordingly" — specify what was changed
3. **No location references**: "We updated the Discussion" — specify page and line numbers
4. **Ignoring the editor**: Editor comments carry more weight than reviewer comments
5. **Promising but not delivering**: Saying you made a change but forgetting to make it
6. **Defensive tone**: "The reviewer is incorrect" — instead, "We respectfully differ..."
7. **Copy-pasting the same response**: Each point deserves a specific response
